Our Story

Ancient Dwellings in the Mountain Kingdom

Nestled in the rolling highlands of Teyateyaneng in Lesotho's Berea District, Kome Caves is one of Africa's most extraordinary inhabited cave settlements. These remarkable sandstone overhangs have sheltered Basotho families since the early 19th century — a living heritage site unlike any other on the continent.

The caves are surrounded by pristine natural springs that tumble through the landscape, creating a lush and serene environment. Here, ancient rock art meets vibrant cultural tradition, and the wisdom of the Basotho people echoes through every stone.

Kome Caves is more than a heritage site — it is a community that breathes, dances, and shares its story with every visitor who walks through its ancient entrance.

Visitor Information

Getting There

Location & Access

Kome Caves is located in Teyateyaneng (TY), Berea District, Lesotho — approximately 42 km north of Maseru via the Main North Road (A1). Well signposted from the town centre. A 4×4 vehicle is recommended for the final approach.

Opening Hours

When to Visit

Monday – Sunday: 08:00 – 17:00

Best seasons: Spring (Sept–Nov) and Autumn (Mar–May) for mild weather. Summer brings lush green surrounds. Winter evenings are spectacular for camping under the stars.

Admission

Fees & Guided Tours

Guided tours are available daily. Entrance fees apply for adults, students, and children. Camping fees are charged per night per person. Cultural workshops (weaving, cooking, dance) are available at an additional fee — advance booking recommended for groups.

What to Bring

Essential Packing List

Comfortable walking shoes for uneven cave terrain · Layered clothing (highlands weather changes quickly) · Camera · Sunscreen & hat · Water bottle · Respectful attire when engaging with residents — modest dress is appreciated.
